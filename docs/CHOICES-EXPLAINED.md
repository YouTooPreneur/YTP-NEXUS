The short list stays the official record; this guide only explains it.

## How to use this guide

- **What these are:** while the 184 screens of YTP Nexus were being drawn, Claude had to decide many small things on its own: times, limits, who can see what. The screens already work that way. Before Nexus is built, someone who knows the business should confirm each one.
- **You do not have to answer all of them.** Answer the ones you are sure about. Anything left blank stays as it is now.
- **Who should answer:** each choice says who knows best: the **Owner** (money, prices, company policy), the **manager** (daily work, staff routine, clients), **both together**, or the **CA** (tax and labour rules).
- **Nothing here is permanent.** Most of these can still be changed later inside Nexus, without new building work.
- **How to reply:** fill the Excel sheet "YTP Nexus - Choices answer sheet" (Keep, Change or Not sure on each row), or write one line, for example "47: send at 10:00, skip holidays · 68: Keep".
- **The examples** use the sample clients and staff of the screens (Sharma Dental Care, Kaveri Hospitals, Karthik, Arun and others). They are not real records.

## Money from clients: bills, reminders and pay links

### 10 · How bill and receipt numbers look

**Answered 22 Oct:** Keep

**Who should answer:** Owner · **Suggestion:** Keep. The papers already carry the YouTooPreneur name, the short numbers fit the 16-character limit, and the CA checks the format before Nexus is used for real anyway.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | Every invoice, credit note (a paper that reduces or cancels an invoice), payment receipt, proposal and Academy certificate gets its own running number. This is about how those numbers look and when they start again from 1. |
| **Example** | Today the next client invoice is YTP/26-27/0046, the next credit note is CN/26-27/004 and the next receipt is RC/26-27/0058. The next proposal is Q-2026-025 and the next Academy certificate is YTA-C/2026/0019. |
| **What Nexus does now** | Client invoices start with YTP (Academy invoices with YTA). Credit notes start with CN and receipts with RC. Invoices, credit notes and receipts start again from 1 every 1 April, when the new financial year begins. Proposals start again every 1 January. Certificates never start again. |
| **The other option** | Put YTP at the front of every number, for example YTP-CN/26-27/0004 for a credit note. |
| **If you keep it** | Numbers stay short, and anyone can tell at a glance what kind of paper it is. Nothing changes for staff or clients. |
| **If you change it** | Good: every paper clearly shows it comes from YTP. Bad: the example above is 17 characters long, and these numbers may have at most 16, so it would need a shorter form, such as YTP-CN/26-27/004. |
| **Who it affects** | The owner, the accountant, clients (they see these numbers on their papers), students (on certificates). |

### 18 · How payment reminders reach clients

**Answered 22 Oct:** Change: payment reminders go by both email and WhatsApp

**Who should answer:** Manager can answer · **Suggestion:** Keep. The invoice itself already goes by both email and WhatsApp, so one friendly WhatsApp reminder is usually enough.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | When a client has not paid, Nexus sends them a polite reminder. This is about which way it goes: WhatsApp, email, or both. |
| **Example** | Greenleaf Organics owes ₹1,416. Their reminder went on WhatsApp this morning at 10:40 (to a test number, because nothing reaches real clients yet). If that WhatsApp had failed, the same reminder would have gone by email instead. |
| **What Nexus does now** | Payment reminders go on WhatsApp first, and by email only if WhatsApp fails. Invoices and renewal notices go by both email and WhatsApp. If a client has asked for one way only (for example 'email only'), their wish always wins. |
| **The other option** | Send every payment reminder by both email and WhatsApp. |
| **If you keep it** | Clients get one gentle reminder in one place, which feels less pushy. Email is the backup. |
| **If you change it** | Good: a reminder is harder to miss, and there is an email record for the accountant. Bad: the client gets the same message twice, which can feel like pressure, especially for clients who usually pay on time. |
| **Who it affects** | Clients, the accountant, managers who handle client calls. |

### 37 · When the owner must call a late payer

**Answered 22 Oct:** Keep

**Who should answer:** Owner and manager together · **Suggestion:** Keep. Day 21 gives both reminders a chance to work first, and slow but steady payers like Kaveri can already have their own later day.

| | |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | If a client still has not paid after the reminders, Nexus gives the owner a task to phone them. This is about how many days after the invoice that happens. |
| **Example** | Anbu Textiles' invoice (Mr. S. Ramesh) was dated 28 Aug. Reminders went on day 7 and day 15, and on day 21 (18 Sep) the owner got the 'call the client' task. ₹28,000 is still owed after 34 days. Kaveri Hospitals has its own day 30, because they usually pay after the second reminder. |
| **What Nexus does now** | Reminders go on day 7 and day 15 after the invoice date. On day 21 the owner gets a 'call the client' task. Each client can have their own day instead. There are never late fees. |
| **The other option** | Make the usual call day 14 or day 30 instead of day 21. |
| **If you keep it** | The call comes one week after the second reminder, so most clients have had two reminders before the owner phones. |
| **If you change it** | Day 14: the call comes before the second reminder (day 15), so money may come in sooner, but some calls would be ones a reminder could have handled. Day 30: fewer calls for the owner, but late money waits longer, and the aim is invoices paid within 15 days. |
| **Who it affects** | The owner (who makes the calls), the accountant, clients. |

**Answered 22 Oct:** Keep

| | |
|---|---|
